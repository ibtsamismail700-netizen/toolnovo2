Top 10 Free PDF Tools for Small Business in 2027

PDF is the format a small business lives in. Invoices go out as PDFs, contracts come back as PDFs, and quotes, receipts, forms and price lists are all PDF too. The problem is that the software most people associate with PDF work costs a monthly subscription — and a small business does not need one for a job that takes two minutes.

Free PDF tools now cover the main jobs: merging, splitting, compressing, converting, signing and filling in forms. Below are the ten best free options for 2027 and where each free plan stops. One honest note first: most free online PDF tools upload your file to their server. For everyday documents that is fine — but never upload contracts with bank details or client data you would not want on someone else's server. For sensitive files, use an offline tool from this list.

1. Adobe Acrobat online tools

The company that invented PDF offers a solid free tier on the web: compress a PDF, merge several into one, convert PDF to Word or an image to PDF — without installing anything. You get a small number of free uses, and the results are reliable because these are the reference tools. It is the safest first stop when a file must come out exactly right, such as a client proposal where a broken layout would look unprofessional.

2. Smallpdf

Smallpdf is the most polished all-in-one online PDF site, with more than twenty tools in one place: compress, merge, split, convert, sign, protect and edit. The free plan gives you a couple of tasks a day, enough for a business that touches PDFs a few times a week. Its interface is the easiest here — drag a file in, pick a tool, download the result.

3. iLovePDF

iLovePDF is Smallpdf's closest rival and the better choice if you work in batches. The free plan lets you process several files at once, merge a folder of invoices into a single PDF, or split a long document by page ranges. It also has a desktop app that keeps files on your own computer.

4. PDF24 Tools

PDF24 is the generous one: its online tools are free with no daily task limit and no account needed, and it also offers a free desktop creator for Windows that works fully offline. The range is wide — merge, split, compress, convert, add page numbers, add a watermark, protect with a password. The interface is plainer, but for daily PDF work, unlimited free use matters more than looks.

5. Sejda

Sejda is the best free option when you need to edit the text inside a PDF — fix a typo in a quote, change a price on a price list, add a paragraph to a letter — rather than just shuffle pages around. The free plan covers a few documents per day with a file-size limit, and files are deleted from its servers automatically after a few hours.

6. PDFescape

PDFescape is a free online editor that runs entirely in the browser: add text, white out a line, draw, highlight, add form fields and insert your signature, then download the finished file. The free version covers files up to 10 MB and 100 pages, plenty for contracts, application forms and order sheets.

7. Canva

Canva is not a PDF tool first, but it is one of the best free ways to make a PDF look good. Design a price list, a menu, a flyer or an invoice template in Canva's free plan and export it as a PDF, or upload an existing PDF and edit its pages as designs. For a business that cares how its documents look — a café menu, a salon price list, a branded quote — Canva does what no plain PDF editor can.

8. PDF-XChange Editor

PDF-XChange Editor is a free desktop program for Windows, and the right pick if you want a proper offline editor rather than a website. The free version reads, annotates, highlights, stamps and fills in forms, and it opens large files quickly. Some advanced features add a watermark until you pay, but the core editing is genuinely free and your files never leave your computer.

9. Foxit PDF Reader

Foxit's free Reader is a fast, lightweight alternative to Adobe Reader for viewing, printing and commenting on PDFs, and its free online tools cover converting and compressing too. It is a good install for every office computer simply because it opens files faster and uses less memory.

10. LibreOffice Draw

LibreOffice Draw is completely free, open source and offline, and it can open a PDF and let you edit its text and objects page by page. It is clumsier than a dedicated PDF editor and it reflows some complex layouts, so it is not the tool for a polished client document. But for internal jobs — correcting a form, updating an old price list whose original file is lost — it costs nothing and never uploads a thing.

Common mistakes to avoid with free PDF tools

  • Uploading sensitive documents to a free website. Bank details, signed contracts and client records should be handled offline — PDF24, PDF-XChange Editor or LibreOffice Draw — not on a server you do not control.
  • Paying for features you already have free. Merging, splitting and compressing are free almost everywhere. Only pay if you edit PDFs every day or need redaction or batch OCR.
  • Compressing a PDF so hard it becomes unreadable. Heavy compression blurs scanned text and small print. Compress in steps and check the result before sending it to a client.
  • Forgetting the original file. Keep the original Word or design file, and treat the PDF as the finished copy, not the master.
  • Using a random converter site from a search result. Unknown PDF sites can inject adverts or malware. Stick to the established names in this list.

How to choose the right free PDF tool

Match the tool to the job. If you only merge invoices and compress attachments once or twice a week, Smallpdf or Adobe's free online tools are enough and nothing needs installing. If PDFs are a daily job, PDF24's unlimited free plan will save you from hitting a daily limit at the worst moment. If you edit text inside PDFs, Sejda is the one free online editor that does it properly. If privacy matters — contracts, tenders, personal data — pick an offline tool: PDF-XChange Editor or LibreOffice Draw.

Key takeaways

  • You do not need a paid subscription for everyday PDF work — merging, splitting, compressing, signing and form-filling are all free.
  • PDF24 is the best unlimited free option; Smallpdf is the easiest; Sejda is the best for editing text inside a PDF.
  • Never upload sensitive documents to a free online tool — use an offline editor for contracts and client data.
  • Keep the original document as the master and treat the PDF as the finished copy.

Frequently asked questions

Are free PDF tools safe to use?

The established tools in this list are safe for everyday documents, and most delete your files from their servers within hours. Still, do not upload bank details or other people's personal data.

Can I really edit a PDF for free?

Yes, for simple edits. Sejda and PDFescape let you change text, add notes and fill in forms in the browser for free, and PDF-XChange Editor and LibreOffice Draw do the same offline. Full professional editing, such as reflowing long documents, is where paid tools still earn their price.

What is the best completely free PDF tool with no limits?

PDF24 Tools has no daily task limit on its free online tools and a free offline desktop creator. LibreOffice Draw is also fully free with no limits at all.

Can I sign a PDF for free?

Yes. Smallpdf, iLovePDF, Sejda, PDFescape and Foxit all let you draw or type a signature onto a PDF for free. A simple drawn signature is fine for most everyday business documents.
