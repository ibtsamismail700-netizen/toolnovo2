How to Speed Up a Slow Website: 10 Free Ways

To speed up a slow website, you rarely need a rebuild or paid tools — you need to remove the weight. Most slow sites share the same few problems: oversized images, too many scripts, and no caching. Work through these ten free fixes in order and most sites become visibly faster within an hour.

Fix the images first (fixes 1–3)

1. Compress every image. Images are typically 50–70% of a page's weight. Run them through an image compressor at around 80% quality. 2. Resize to display size — never serve a 4000px photo in an 800px slot. 3. Use modern formats — WebP instead of JPG/PNG where possible. Our full walkthrough is in how to compress images without losing quality.

Cut what the page carries (fixes 4–6)

4. Delete unused plugins and scripts. Every plugin, chat widget, tracker and social embed adds requests and code. If it does not earn its place, it goes. 5. Reduce third-party fonts — one family, two weights, is enough for almost every site; load them efficiently and let text show in a fallback font while they arrive. 6. Minify and combine where your platform allows — smaller CSS and JavaScript files download and parse faster.

Serve pages smarter (fixes 7–10)

7. Turn on caching so repeat visitors load stored copies instead of downloading everything again. 8. Use a free CDN tier to serve files from locations near your visitors and add modern compression (Brotli/Gzip). 9. Lazy-load below-the-fold images and videos so the visible part of the page renders first. 10. Check your hosting — if the server itself responds slowly (a high “time to first byte”), no page fix can compensate; our web hosting guide explains when an upgrade is genuinely the answer.

Measure before and after

Run a free speed test (PageSpeed Insights is the standard) before you start and after each round of fixes. Chase the specific warnings it names for your pages rather than a perfect score — and run our On-Page SEO Checker too, since slow, heavy pages and weak on-page SEO often travel together.

Key takeaways

  • Images are the biggest, cheapest win: compress, resize, convert.
  • Every script and plugin must justify its weight.
  • Caching and a CDN make repeat visits and distant visitors fast.

Frequently asked questions

How fast should a website load?

Aim for the main content to appear within about 2.5 seconds on a mobile connection — that is the threshold search engines use for a “good” experience.

Does speed affect Google rankings?

Yes, as one factor among many, and it strongly affects whether visitors stay. Fast pages convert better even where rankings do not move.

What is the single biggest speed fix?

For most sites, image weight. It is common to cut a page's total size by half with image fixes alone.

My speed score is fine on desktop but bad on mobile — why?

Mobile tests simulate slower networks and weaker processors, so heavy images and scripts hurt far more. Optimise for the mobile result; desktop will follow.
