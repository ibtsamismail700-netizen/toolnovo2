Top 10 Free Budgeting Apps in 2027

⚠ Please note

General information only — not financial advice. Figures in this article are examples to explain how the maths works. Your own rates, fees and terms will differ, so confirm everything with your lender, insurer or a licensed adviser before you decide.

A budget only works if you actually look at it. That is the problem with most budgeting advice: a spreadsheet you build once, admire for a week, then never open again. A good budgeting app fixes the boring part — it tracks the numbers, shows where the money went, and warns you before a category runs dry. The good news is you do not need to pay for that: several solid budgeting apps still offer a genuinely free plan in 2027.

Here are the ten best and how to pick the right one. One honest note first: Mint, the most famous budgeting app of the last decade, shut down in 2024 — if you want a Mint replacement, start with numbers 1, 3 and 4.

1. Goodbudget

Goodbudget is the digital version of the envelope method: you divide your money into envelopes — groceries, rent, transport, fun — and spend only what is in each one. The free plan covers one account, two devices and up to 20 envelopes. It is manual entry, which sounds like a drawback until you realise typing in each expense is exactly what makes you notice it.

2. EveryDollar

EveryDollar is built around zero-based budgeting: every dollar of income gets a job before the month starts, so income minus planned spending equals zero. The free version is manual entry with unlimited categories and a clean layout that takes minutes to learn. Paid plans add bank syncing, but the free version is a complete budgeting system on its own.

3. PocketGuard

PocketGuard answers one question better than any other app here: how much can I safely spend right now? It connects to your accounts, subtracts bills, savings goals and spending, and shows your "In My Pocket" figure — the money genuinely free to spend. The free plan covers account syncing, bill tracking and spending categories.

4. Empower Personal Dashboard

Empower (formerly Personal Capital) is free, and it is the strongest free option if you care about the whole picture rather than just this month's spending. It pulls bank accounts, cards, loans and investments into one dashboard and tracks your net worth over time. Its budgeting tools are simpler than a dedicated app, but nothing else free shows your finances this clearly.

5. Honeydue

Honeydue is built for couples who share money. Both partners link their accounts, see shared bills and balances together, and can chat about any transaction inside the app — which ends the "did you pay the electricity bill?" conversation. The app is free, with optional paid extras, and handles joint and individual accounts side by side.

6. Fudget

Fudget is deliberately simple: a list of income, a list of expenses, and a balance. No bank syncing, no charts, no categories to configure — you type numbers in and it does the maths. The free version covers the core budgeting, and the interface is so plain there is nothing to learn. Best for a simple side budget such as a holiday.

7. Spendee

Spendee makes budgeting visual: colourful wallets, clear charts and a shared-wallet feature for splitting costs with family or friends. The free plan includes manual wallets, expense tracking and basic budgeting, with bank syncing on paid plans. It also handles multiple currencies well.

8. Monefy

Monefy is a fast expense tracker with a clean, icon-based interface: tap an icon, type the amount, done. The free version covers unlimited manual entries and categories, plus a clear chart of where the money goes. It does not sync with banks — for many people a feature, since your data stays on your device.

9. Rocket Money

Rocket Money combines budgeting with subscription tracking: it finds your recurring charges, flags the ones you forgot about, and helps you cancel them. The free tier includes budgeting, spending tracking and bill reminders, and most people find at least one forgotten charge in the subscription overview.

10. Zeta

Zeta is another strong free option for couples, with a different angle from Honeydue: it is designed around joint goals as well as joint bills. Couples can track shared spending, split bills by custom percentages, and save together towards a named goal, all in one free account. Individual accounts stay private unless you choose to share them.

How to choose the right budgeting app

The best app is the one you will still open in three months, so choose for your habits, not for the feature list:

  • Pick your method first. Envelope budgeting, zero-based budgeting and simple tracking are different systems. Try Goodbudget for envelopes or EveryDollar for zero-based for one month each.
  • Be honest about manual entry. Bank syncing is convenient, but manual entry makes you notice spending. If you have quit budgeting apps before because you stopped logging, choose one with syncing, such as PocketGuard or Empower.
  • Budgeting together? Choose together. A couples app only works if both partners use it. Honeydue and Zeta are built for two people.
  • Check the free tier first. Free plans change. Confirm on the app's own pricing page that the features you need are in the free tier before you set everything up.
  • Start with three categories, not thirty. Begin with housing, food and one "everything else" category.

Common mistakes that break a budgeting app

  • Setting a fantasy budget. If you spend $600 a month on food, budgeting $300 does not save $300 — it just guarantees a red warning in week two. Budget from last month's real spending.
  • Forgetting irregular costs. Car insurance, annual subscriptions and holidays wreck monthly budgets because they are not monthly. Add one category for irregular costs and fund it monthly.
  • Tracking but never reviewing. The app collects the data; yours is to look at it. Ten minutes a week catches a drifting category early.
  • Paying before testing the free tier. Every app on this list has a usable free tier. Run it for a full month first; upgrade only when a paid feature clearly saves you more than it costs.

A useful starting point for your numbers is the 50/30/20 rule — needs, wants and savings in three buckets. The 50/30/20 Budget Rule Calculator turns your own income into the three amounts in seconds, and the Savings Goal Calculator shows how long a target takes at your monthly amount.

Frequently asked questions

Are free budgeting apps really free?

Yes — every app in this list has a free tier you can use indefinitely. What varies is which features are free: manual-entry apps are usually free in full, while apps with bank syncing often keep advanced features for paid plans.

Is it safe to connect my bank account to a budgeting app?

Reputable apps connect through regulated aggregation services using read-only access, so the app can see transactions but cannot move money. Stick to well-known apps, use two-factor authentication, and never give an app your online banking password directly. Our guide to two-factor authentication explains how to lock the account down.

What is the best free budgeting app overall?

There is no single winner. Goodbudget is the best free choice for envelope budgeting, EveryDollar for zero-based budgeting, PocketGuard if you want one safe-to-spend number, and Empower if you want budgeting plus your full financial picture.

Do I need a budgeting app if I already use a spreadsheet?

No — a spreadsheet you actually update beats an app you ignore. An app earns its place by removing friction: automatic tracking, alerts before a category runs out, and your budget on your phone at the shop.

Key takeaways

  • Ten solid apps offer a genuinely free plan, from simple trackers such as Fudget and Monefy to full systems such as Goodbudget and EveryDollar.
  • Choose by method and habit — envelopes, zero-based, or one safe-to-spend number — and pick couples apps only if both partners will use them.
  • Budget from your real spending, review the numbers for ten minutes each week, and give any app a full month before deciding whether it works.
