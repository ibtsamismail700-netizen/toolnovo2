How Google AdSense Approval Works: What New Sites Must Fix First

⚠ Please note

General information only — not financial or tax advice. AdSense policies and approval criteria change over time, so always check the official Google AdSense programme policies for the current rules before acting on anything in this guide. Earnings from advertising vary widely between sites and are never guaranteed.

AdSense approval is the gate between building a website and earning from it, and for new sites it can feel mysterious: some are approved in days, others are rejected repeatedly with vague messages. In practice, Google checks a knowable list of things — content quality, site completeness and policy compliance. This guide explains how AdSense approval works and what to fix first.

What Google looks for before AdSense approval

  • Original, substantial content — pages that genuinely help visitors, written by you, long enough to be useful. Thin pages, copied text and collections of embeds are the top rejection cause.
  • A complete, working site — clear navigation, no broken links, no “under construction” pages, and a design that works on mobile.
  • The essential pages — About, Contact and a proper Privacy Policy that discloses advertising cookies. Missing legal pages signal an unfinished site.
  • Policy-safe topics and behaviour — no copied or prohibited content, no misleading buttons, no encouraging accidental ad clicks.

The fixes that solve most rejections

1. Deepen thin content. Every important page should stand on its own: a clear purpose, real information, and enough depth that a visitor leaves satisfied. On ToolNovo, every tool page carries a full written guide beneath the tool for exactly this reason. 2. Publish the essential pages and link them in the footer. 3. Fix navigation and broken links — reviewers click around like visitors. 4. Check readability — clear headings and short paragraphs (our Readability Checker helps) signal a site made for humans. 5. Run an on-page check with the SEO Checker so titles and descriptions are in place.

What happens during review

After you add Google's verification code, reviewers (automated and human) crawl your site. Reviews take from a day to a few weeks. If you are rejected, the message names a category — “low value content” is the most common — and you may reapply after fixing the issues. Reapplying without changes simply repeats the result.

After approval: staying approved

Approval is not permanent immunity. Keep publishing original content, never click your own ads or ask others to, place ads so they do not trick visitors, and keep the Privacy Policy accurate. Sites that treat AdSense as a long-term partnership with their readers keep their accounts; sites that chase quick clicks lose them. Understanding how search engines work helps you grow the honest traffic that makes ads worthwhile in the first place.

Key takeaways

  • Approval rests on original content, a complete site and policy compliance.
  • “Low value content” usually means thin, copied or unfinished pages — fix depth first.
  • Essential pages (About, Contact, Privacy Policy) are not optional decoration.

Frequently asked questions

How much traffic do I need for AdSense approval?

There is no official traffic minimum. Content quality and site completeness decide approval; traffic decides earnings afterwards.

How many pages or posts should a new site have?

No fixed number is published, but a site with a solid body of complete, useful pages — rather than a handful of stubs — presents far better. Quality and completeness beat raw count.

Why was I rejected for “low value content”?

Usually thin pages, content copied or lightly rewritten from elsewhere, or pages that exist only to hold ads. Add genuine depth and originality, then reapply.

Can I apply again after a rejection?

Yes, once you have fixed the named issues. There is no penalty for reapplying after real improvements.
