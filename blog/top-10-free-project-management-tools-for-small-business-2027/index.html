Top 10 Free Project Management Tools for Small Business in 2027

⚠ Please note

General information only — not financial advice. Figures in this article are examples to explain how the maths works. Your own rates, fees and terms will differ, so confirm everything with your lender, insurer or a licensed adviser before you decide.

A project management tool turns a messy to-do list into work your whole team can see: who is doing what, when it is due, and what is finished. Without one, tasks hide in chat messages, deadlines slip, and the same job gets done twice — or not at all.

The good news: you do not need a budget to start. In 2027 the biggest project management tools all offer a genuinely free plan — free forever, not a 14-day trial — and several are good enough to run a real small business on. Every free plan has a different limit, though, so this guide compares ten of them side by side.

1. ClickUp — best overall free plan

ClickUp's free plan is the most generous here: unlimited users, unlimited tasks and unlimited projects, with list, board and calendar views, docs, whiteboards and basic time tracking included. No other free plan here lets a whole team work together without paying a cent.

The catch is storage: just 60 MB across the whole workspace, so files must live in Google Drive or Dropbox. Paid plans start at $9 per user per month.

2. Trello — simplest visual boards

Trello is kanban distilled: boards of lists, cards you drag from To Do to Done, and nothing you must configure first. Anyone on your team can learn it in a minute. The free plan gives 10 boards per workspace, unlimited cards, up to 10 collaborators and 250 automation runs a month.

The limits are the board count and the 10 MB cap on each file — a team with many projects will run out of boards fast. Paid plans start at $5 per user per month, the cheapest here.

3. Asana — clean task lists for pairs

Asana's free Personal plan is the most disciplined home on this list: unlimited tasks and projects, list, board and calendar views and status updates, with a 100 MB limit per file. Every task has one person and one due date.

The catch is the two-user cap: it is a plan for a pair, not a team, and Timeline, Gantt charts and custom fields are all paid features. Paid plans start at $10.99 per user per month.

4. Notion — docs and projects in one place

Notion is different from every other tool here: notes, docs, wikis and project boards live side by side in one workspace. The free plan gives one person unlimited pages and blocks, databases in five views, 10 guest invites and 5 MB file uploads.

The catch: it is built for one person, team features are limited, and page history only goes back 7 days. Paid plans start at $10 per user per month.

5. monday.com — trying visual boards first

monday.com is the most visual tool on this list: colourful boards, more than 200 templates, kanban and calendar views, and a polished interface. The free plan covers up to 2 seats with 3 boards and 3 docs — enough to test whether the monday way of working suits you.

The catch: the free plan is a trial, not a home — 3 boards will not run a real business, and automations and integrations are paid. Paid plans start at $9 per seat per month with a 3-seat minimum, so the smallest paid bill is $27 a month.

6. Wrike — unlimited seats, basic tracking

Wrike's free plan is the quiet bargain here: unlimited users, 2 GB of storage and 200 active tasks, with board view, file sharing and team activity included. It is the only free plan besides ClickUp that lets a whole team in without a seat cap.

The catch is the 200 active-task limit — a busy team will hit it within weeks — and dashboards and Gantt charts are paid. Paid plans start at $10 per user per month.

7. Jira — software teams and sprints

Jira is the standard for software teams: scrum and kanban boards, sprint planning, backlogs and issue tracking built for developers. The free plan covers up to 10 users with 2 GB of storage and 100 automation runs a month.

The catch: Jira is built for software, so non-technical teams often find it heavy, and permissions are limited on the free plan. Paid plans start at $8.60 per user per month.

8. Zoho Projects — classic project structure

Zoho Projects is the classic, no-surprises option: projects, milestones, task lists, subtasks, Gantt charts and time tracking, with 5 users, 3 projects and 5 GB of storage on the free plan. It is the obvious pick if your business already uses Zoho Books, Mail or CRM, because the suite shares the same data.

The catch is the 3-project limit — a business juggling five or six clients will outgrow it quickly. Paid plans start at about $4 per user per month, among the cheapest here.

9. Todoist — simple personal task lists

Todoist is the simplest tool on this list, and that is the point: a clean task list with due dates, priorities, labels and reminders, on every device, with no setup at all. The free plan gives 5 projects, 5 collaborators per project, 3 filters and one week of activity history.

The catch is the 5-project limit and the lack of boards or team features — Todoist organises your own work, it does not run a team. Paid plans start at $4 per user per month.

10. Teamwork.com — client work and billing

Teamwork.com is built for agencies and service businesses: projects, tasks, milestones and time tracking sit next to client billing, so you can see which projects make money and which do not. The free plan covers up to 5 users and 5 projects, with 100 automation runs a month.

The catch is the 5-project cap and the price: paid plans start at $10.99 per user per month, and workload planning and profitability reports sit on higher tiers.

Common mistakes when choosing a free project management tool

First, do not choose on the free plan alone: check the paid price you will face when you outgrow it — a plan that jumps to $27 a month because of a 3-seat minimum hurts more than one that starts at $4 a user. Second, count your real team: a 2-user free plan is useless if three people need access. Third, check the project cap, not just the user cap: Trello's 10 boards and Zoho Projects' 3 projects run out fast. Finally, confirm you can export your data: some free plans limit how often you can take your own projects out.

Frequently asked questions

Is a free project management tool really free forever? Yes, for every tool in this list: the free plan has no time limit and asks for no credit card. What is limited is users, projects, storage or features — not the calendar.

Which free project management tool is best for a team of ten? ClickUp or Wrike — both allow unlimited free users. Jira and Trello cap free plans at 10 people, so a team of ten sits exactly at their limit. Asana, monday.com and Notion cap free plans at one or two people, so a team of ten would need to pay.

Can I move my projects to a paid plan later? Yes. Every tool here lets you upgrade in place and keep your tasks, boards and history. Moving to a different tool is possible by export — but check the free plan's export limits first.

Key takeaways

  • ClickUp is the best free project management tool for most small businesses; Trello is the simplest; Wrike is the best free option for a larger team with no budget.
  • Check the paid price and the seat minimum first — the first paid step ranges from $4 to $10.99 per user per month, and monday.com bills a minimum of 3 seats.
  • Count users, projects and storage before you commit, test with a real project, and confirm you can export your data.
