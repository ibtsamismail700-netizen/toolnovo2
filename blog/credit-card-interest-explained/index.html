How Credit Card Interest Works (and Why Minimum Payments Cost You)

⚠ Please note

General information only — not financial advice. Figures in this article are examples to explain how the maths works. Your own rates, fees and terms will differ, so confirm everything with your lender, insurer or a licensed adviser before you decide.

Credit card interest is some of the most expensive borrowing most people ever use — and the least understood. Cards advertise a yearly rate, but interest is actually calculated daily, and the “minimum payment” on your statement is designed to keep the debt alive for years. Once you see the maths, you will never look at a minimum payment the same way.

How credit card interest is calculated

Your card has an APR — an annual percentage rate, often around 20–30%. The issuer divides that by 365 to get a daily rate, then charges it on your average daily balance. In plain terms: owe $3,000 at 24% APR and you are paying roughly $2 a day, about $60 a month, in interest alone — before you have bought anything new.

There is one escape hatch: the grace period. If you pay your full statement balance by the due date, purchases usually cost no interest at all. The moment you carry a balance, that protection typically disappears and new purchases start accruing interest immediately.

Why minimum payments cost so much

A minimum payment is usually a small percentage of the balance — often 1–3% — plus interest. Because it shrinks as the balance shrinks, the debt fades painfully slowly:

Example: a $3,000 balance at 24% APR, paying only the minimum (interest + 1% of the balance), takes roughly 10 years to clear and costs around $3,300 in interest — more than the original purchase. Pay a fixed $150 a month instead and the same debt is gone in about 2 years, with roughly $850 of interest.

How to pay less credit card interest

  • Pay a fixed amount, not a percentage — set it above the minimum and keep it the same every month.
  • Pay more than once a month — interest is charged on the daily balance, so earlier payments reduce it.
  • Stop new spending on the card while you clear it, so the balance only moves one way.
  • Ask about a lower rate — a good payment history sometimes earns a reduction; it costs nothing to ask.
  • Consider the payoff order if you have several cards — our snowball vs avalanche guide compares the two main strategies.

A note on balance-transfer offers

A 0% balance-transfer card can pause interest and help you clear debt faster, but only if you clear it inside the promotional window, never miss a payment, and account for the transfer fee. Treat it as a tool with a deadline, not a solution by itself.

Key takeaways

  • Credit card interest is charged daily on your balance; the APR divided by 365 is the daily rate.
  • Minimum payments shrink with the balance, stretching debt for years and multiplying its cost.
  • A fixed monthly payment above the minimum is the simplest way to pay far less interest.

Frequently asked questions

When is credit card interest charged?

Interest accrues daily on any balance you carry past the due date. Paying the full statement balance on time usually means no interest on purchases at all.

Why did my balance barely move after a payment?

Because most of a minimum payment is absorbed by that month's interest. As the balance falls, a growing share of each payment reaches the balance itself.

Is it better to pay the statement balance or the full balance?

Paying the statement balance by the due date avoids interest. Paying the full current balance also covers recent purchases — either way, avoid paying only the minimum.
