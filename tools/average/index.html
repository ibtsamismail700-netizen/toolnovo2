About the Average Calculator

The average calculator finds the mean of any list of numbers and goes further: median, mode, sum, smallest, largest and range. Those extra statistics matter, because a single mean can hide a lot — in exam scores, salaries, house prices and sports stats alike.

How to use the Average Calculator — step by step

  1. Type or paste your numbers, separated by commas, spaces or new lines.
  2. Read the mean as the headline result.
  3. Check the median and mode below to see whether the mean is being pulled by outliers.

Worked example

For the scores 85, 92, 78, 92, 88, 95, 71: the mean is 85.9, the median is 88, the mode is 92, the sum is 601 and the range is 24 (from 71 to 95). The mean sits below the median because the 71 pulls it down.

When to use it

Use it for grade averages, monthly spending, sports statistics, survey results and any list where 'typical' matters more than total.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Reporting only the mean when one extreme value skews it — check the median too.
  • Averaging averages: combining two group means without weighting by group size gives the wrong answer.
  • Including a total or a zero placeholder in the list by accident.

Average Calculator — frequently asked questions

What is the difference between mean and median?

The mean adds everything and divides by the count. The median is the middle value when sorted. For house prices and salaries, the median is usually the more honest 'typical' figure.

What if there are two modes?

A list can be bimodal. This tool lists every value tied for most frequent, or says 'no mode' when nothing repeats.
