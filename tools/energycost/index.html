About the Appliance Energy Cost Calculator

The appliance energy cost calculator turns a wattage label and your electricity price into the numbers that matter: what a device costs per day, month and year. With energy prices a top household worry, it finds the appliances quietly inflating your bill.

How to use the Appliance Energy Cost Calculator — step by step

  1. Find the wattage on the appliance label, plug or manual (or use typical values: fridge ~150W, washing machine ~500W per cycle hour, electric heater 1,500–2,000W, gaming PC 300–500W).
  2. Enter hours used per day.
  3. Enter your electricity price per kWh from your latest bill.
  4. Read the daily, monthly and yearly costs and hunt the surprises.

Worked example

A 2,000W space heater used 4 hours a day at $0.16/kWh uses 8 kWh daily — $1.28 a day, $38 a month, $467 a year. The fridge next to it, running all day at 150W, costs about a third as much.

When to use it

Use it when a bill spikes, before buying a heater, hot tub, tumble dryer or mining/gaming rig, or when comparing appliance running costs in a shop.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Using the maximum wattage for cycling appliances — fridges and air conditioners switch on and off, so real use is often 40–60% of the label.
  • Forgetting standby: TVs, consoles and chargers sipping 5–10W all year still add up to real money.
  • Using a headline price per kWh that excludes delivery charges and taxes — use the all-in rate from your bill.

Appliance Energy Cost Calculator — frequently asked questions

Where do I find my price per kWh?

Divide a bill's total electricity charge by kWh used, or find the unit rate line: US bills vary by state, the UK price cap is around 25p, and Canadian rates vary by province.

What uses the most electricity in a home?

Heating and cooling first, then water heating, tumble dryers, ovens and hot tubs — anything that makes heat.
