About the Retirement Savings Calculator

The retirement savings calculator projects what your workplace pension, 401(k), RRSP or personal investments could grow to by the age you stop work, assuming a steady return and monthly contributions. It is an illustration, not a forecast — but it shows the two levers that matter most: starting early and contributing consistently.

How to use the Retirement Savings Calculator — step by step

  1. Enter your age now and your planned retirement age.
  2. Enter what you have saved so far and your monthly contribution, including any employer match.
  3. Enter an expected annual return — 5–7% is a common planning range for a mixed portfolio.
  4. Read the projected pot, what you contributed, and what growth added.

Worked example

A 30-year-old with $25,000 saved, adding $500 a month at 7% until 67, contributes about $247,000 in total — but the pot reaches roughly $1.08 million. Starting the same plan at 40 instead produces roughly half as much.

When to use it

Use it to set a monthly contribution, check whether you are on track, or show a younger family member why starting at 25 beats starting at 35.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Counting on a high return to rescue low contributions — contributions are the lever you control.
  • Forgetting employer matching: an unclaimed match is literally declined pay.
  • Ignoring inflation — a future pot buys less than the same number today; the Inflation Calculator shows how much less.

Retirement Savings Calculator — frequently asked questions

How much should I save?

A common US/UK/Canadian guideline is 12–15% of gross income including employer contributions, adjusted for when you start.

Does this include state pensions or Social Security?

No — it projects only your own savings pot. Government pensions are a separate income layer on top.
