About the What Is My Browser?

What is my browser reads the information your own browser already announces — its name and version, your operating system, screen and window size, language, time zone and the full user-agent string — and lays it out clearly. It is the page support teams ask you to open when something online 'works for them but not for you'.

How to use the What Is My Browser? — step by step

  1. Open the tool — every detail is filled in automatically from your browser.
  2. Find the exact item you need: browser version, OS, screen size or user agent.
  3. Tap Copy next to the user agent, or copy the whole report.
  4. Send it to whoever is helping you, or use it to check your own setup.

Worked example

A site looks broken and support asks for your setup: this page answers in one glance — 'Chrome 126 on Windows 11, screen 1920×1080, window 1340×768, language en-GB' — instead of a guessing game across five settings menus.

When to use it

Use it when reporting a bug, checking whether your browser is up to date, testing responsive layouts against your real window size, or verifying a new device's language and time zone.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Confusing screen size with window size — websites respond to the window; the tool shows both so you can see the difference.
  • Assuming the user agent is always truthful: privacy tools and some browsers deliberately generalise it.
  • Sharing the full report publicly without need — it is low-risk, but it is still your device fingerprint; send it to support, not to strangers.

What Is My Browser? — frequently asked questions

Does ToolNovo collect this information?

No. The details are read and displayed locally by your browser; nothing is sent to or stored by ToolNovo.

Why does my browser name look wrong?

Most modern browsers are built on Chromium, and user-agent strings carry historical names for compatibility. The tool shows the most specific match it can detect.
