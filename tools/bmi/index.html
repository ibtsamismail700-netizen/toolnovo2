About the BMI Calculator

The BMI calculator works out your Body Mass Index — a quick screening number that compares your weight with your height. It is used by doctors and health agencies worldwide as a first check of whether your weight sits in a healthy range. Enter measurements in imperial (feet, inches, pounds) or metric (centimetres, kilograms).

How to use the BMI Calculator — step by step

  1. Choose US imperial or metric units.
  2. Enter your height and your weight.
  3. Read your BMI number and its category: underweight, healthy, overweight or obese.
  4. Check the healthy weight range shown for your exact height.

Worked example

At 5 ft 9 in (175 cm) and 170 lb (77 kg), BMI is 25.1 — just into the overweight band, which starts at 25. The healthy range for that height is about 125–168 lb (57–76 kg), so losing a few pounds crosses back into the healthy band.

When to use it

Use it as a starting point for a weight goal, before a health check, or to track change over time alongside waist size, fitness and how you feel.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Treating BMI as a diagnosis — it cannot tell muscle from fat, so athletes often score 'overweight' while being very healthy.
  • Using it for children — under-18s need age- and sex-specific percentile charts, not adult bands.
  • Measuring height in shoes or guessing weight — small errors move the result noticeably.

BMI Calculator — frequently asked questions

What is a healthy BMI?

For most adults, 18.5 to 24.9 is the healthy range. Below 18.5 is underweight, 25–29.9 overweight, and 30 or above obese.

Is BMI different in the UK, US and Canada?

No — the formula and bands are the same worldwide. Only the units people enter differ.
