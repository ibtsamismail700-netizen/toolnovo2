About the Commute Cost & WFH Savings Calculator

The commute cost and work-from-home savings calculator prices your commute in fuel and fares per day, month and year, then shows what each work-from-home day is worth. Commuting is a cost employers never list in the salary — this makes it visible for job and housing decisions.

How to use the Commute Cost & WFH Savings Calculator — step by step

  1. Enter your round-trip distance and commute days per week.
  2. Enter fuel economy and fuel price, or your daily transit and parking cost.
  3. Enter how many days a week you can work from home.
  4. Read the monthly and yearly commute cost and your WFH saving.

Worked example

A 22-mile round trip at 30 mpg with $3.60 fuel and $12 parking costs about $14.64 a day — roughly $317 a month and $3,800 a year. Working from home two days a week saves about $127 a month, over $1,500 a year, before counting time.

When to use it

Use it when comparing job offers, negotiating hybrid days, choosing where to live, or costing a move further from work.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Counting only fuel — parking, transit passes, tolls and wear often exceed it.
  • Ignoring your time: an hour a day is roughly 10 full working days a year.
  • Assuming a distant cheaper house saves money before pricing the commute that comes with it.

Commute Cost & WFH Savings Calculator — frequently asked questions

What is the full cost per mile of driving?

Motoring organisations such as the AAA estimate all-in costs (fuel, wear, depreciation, insurance share) at several times the fuel-only cost — fuel is the floor, not the total.

Is one WFH day really worth much?

For a typical car commuter, roughly $40–$70 a week per day at home once fuel, parking and fares are counted — this tool uses your numbers.
