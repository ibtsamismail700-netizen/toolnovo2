About the Stopwatch

The stopwatch times anything to hundredths of a second, with lap timing that records each split and the running total. It runs in your browser tab, so it is always one tap away for workouts, cooking, experiments and games — no app install, no sign-up.

How to use the Stopwatch — step by step

  1. Press Start to begin timing.
  2. Press Lap at each interval to record a split.
  3. Press Stop to pause (Resume continues), and Reset to clear everything.

Worked example

Timing four 400 m laps, you might record splits of 1:28.40, 1:31.05, 1:30.62 and 1:27.88 — the lap list shows each split beside the total, instantly revealing that lap 2 was the slow one.

When to use it

Use it for training intervals, swim and run laps, science experiments, speed-cubing, cooking stages and classroom timing.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Leaving the tab in the background on a phone that aggressively freezes tabs — keep the tab visible for long timings.
  • Forgetting to record laps before resetting — reset clears the lap list too.
  • Trusting any stopwatch to the hundredth for official results — reaction time on the button is about ±0.2 seconds.

Timing well: intervals, reaction time and honest splits

A stopwatch is only as good as the routine around it. For interval training, press Lap at the end of every work and rest block instead of stopping the clock — afterwards the lap list shows whether your work intervals faded (a pacing problem) or your rests crept longer (a discipline problem), and the two need different fixes. For cooking, lap each stage of a multi-part recipe — sear, rest, sauce — and you build a repeatable timing card for next time. For speaking practice, lap at each section of a talk to see which part overruns. One honest limit: your thumb is part of the measurement. Human reaction time adds roughly two-tenths of a second of error to both start and stop, so treat hundredths as useful for comparing your own laps, not as laboratory precision.

Stopwatch — frequently asked questions

How accurate is it?

It uses your device clock and displays to 0.01 seconds. The limiting factor is human reaction time, not the timer.

Do laps show split or total?

Both — each lap row shows that lap's own time and the total elapsed time at that point.

Will the stopwatch keep running in the background?

It keeps counting from your device clock, but phones may freeze background tabs to save battery, which can pause the display. For long or important timings, keep the tab visible.
