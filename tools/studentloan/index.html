About the Student Loan Calculator

The student loan calculator turns a borrowed total into a monthly payment, a payoff date and the true interest cost of a degree. It models a standard repayment loan like US federal and private student loans; UK income-contingent loans work differently and are explained in the notes.

How to use the Student Loan Calculator — step by step

  1. Enter the total borrowed and the interest rate.
  2. Enter the repayment term in years — 10 is the US federal standard.
  3. Optionally add an extra monthly payment to see the saving.
  4. Read the payment, total interest and payoff date.

Worked example

Borrowing $35,000 at 5.5% over 10 years costs about $380 a month and $10,600 in interest. Adding $75 extra a month clears it roughly two years early and saves around $1,900.

When to use it

Use it before borrowing, when choosing between loan offers, or when planning extra payments after a pay rise.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Borrowing the maximum offered rather than the amount your expected salary supports.
  • Ignoring interest that accrues while you study on unsubsidised loans.
  • Assuming UK loans behave like this — UK Plan loans are repaid as 9% of income above a threshold and are written off after a set period.

Student Loan Calculator — frequently asked questions

How much student debt is reasonable?

A common rule: total borrowing no more than your expected first-year salary.

Should I overpay or invest?

Compare the loan rate with expected investment returns and keep an emergency fund first — the loan rate is a guaranteed saving.
