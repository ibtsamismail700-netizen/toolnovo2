About the Debt Snowball Payoff Calculator

The debt snowball clears debts from smallest balance to largest, regardless of interest rate. Each cleared debt frees its payment to roll into the next one — the snowball. It costs slightly more interest than the avalanche method, but the quick wins keep people going, and a plan you finish beats a perfect plan you quit.

How to use the Debt Snowball Payoff Calculator — step by step

  1. List up to three debts: balance, minimum payment and APR for each.
  2. Add any extra money you can put toward debt each month.
  3. The tool orders debts smallest-first and simulates every payment.
  4. Read your debt-free date and total interest.

Worked example

Debts of $800 (29.99% APR, $25 minimum), $1,200 (24.99%, $35) and $3,400 (19.99%, $85), plus $150 extra a month, clear in roughly two years — the $800 card disappears in about four months, and its payment joins the attack on the next debt.

When to use it

Use it when you have several cards and loans and need a clear order of attack and a finish line to aim at.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Stopping minimum payments on any debt — every debt gets its minimum, always, or fees and credit damage follow.
  • Adding new spending to a card you are clearing.
  • Ignoring a 0% balance-transfer window that could pause interest while you snowball.

Debt Snowball Payoff Calculator — frequently asked questions

Snowball or avalanche — which is better?

Avalanche (highest APR first) is mathematically cheapest. Snowball is behaviourally strongest. Pick the one you will still be following in month six.

What if a payment does not cover interest?

The tool warns you — raise the extra amount, because the balance grows otherwise.
