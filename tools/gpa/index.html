About the GPA Calculator

The GPA calculator computes your grade point average on the US 4.0 scale, weighted by credit hours, and can fold in your previous cumulative GPA. It is the number colleges, scholarship committees and many US employers screen on — and Canadian students applying to US schools use it too.

How to use the GPA Calculator — step by step

  1. Add each course with its credit hours and letter grade.
  2. Press Add course for more rows, or remove rows you do not need.
  3. Optionally enter your prior cumulative GPA and credits earned so far.
  4. Read this term's GPA, total credits, and your new cumulative GPA.

Worked example

English 101 (3 credits, A = 4.0), Calculus I (4 credits, B+ = 3.3), Biology 110 (3 credits, A− = 3.7) and a 3-credit B course give 45.3 grade points over 13 credits — a term GPA of 3.48.

When to use it

Use it each term to track progress toward honours, scholarship minimums (often 3.0) and graduate school requirements, and to see what a single grade does to your average before results day.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Weighting every course equally — a 4-credit A lifts your GPA far more than a 1-credit A.
  • Using the wrong scale: this is the standard unweighted 4.0 scale; weighted honours/AP scales run to 5.0.
  • Forgetting failed or repeated courses — most schools count them under their own repeat policy, so check yours.

GPA Calculator — frequently asked questions

What GPA do I need for honours?

Commonly cum laude is about 3.5, magna cum laude 3.7 and summa cum laude 3.9, but every school sets its own thresholds.

What is a B+ worth?

3.3 points. The full scale is in the note under the calculator: A = 4.0, A− = 3.7, B = 3.0, C = 2.0, D = 1.0, F = 0.
